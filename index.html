How it works

Four steps. Your experts in the room for all of them.

  1. Vet

    Your staff pick the reports, datasets, and guidelines it may use. Nothing else gets in.

  2. Organize

    Every source is tagged by author, audience, topic, and place, so the most authoritative one wins.

  3. Break

    We attack it with made-up facts, leading questions, and medical asks, in every language it speaks.

  4. Hand over

    It goes live on your site with a playbook. You own it. We stay reachable.

What we promise

Three rules it will not break, even for the people who deploy it.

1

It only says what it can cite.

No approved source, no claim.

2

It says "I don't know" out loud.

Gaps are part of the answer, never papered over.

3

Honest with the public first.

When honesty is inconvenient for the mission, honesty wins. We put that in writing.

Questions we get

What a director asks before saying yes.

What does it cost a partner?

Nothing for the build or the testing. We're grant and donor funded. Hosting afterward is a small cost we walk through with you first.

Who owns it once it's live?

You do. Sources, tagging, tests, configuration, and the playbook are yours. Nothing depends on us staying.

What happens to the questions people ask?

No names or contact details are collected. Questions are logged only so your team can catch bad answers, kept as long as you decide, and never leave your organization.

Which languages?

Whichever your community uses, provided we can test properly in them. We won't ship a language we can't evaluate.

Which AI model is underneath?

Commercial models, swapped when a better or cheaper one appears. The behavior comes from the sources, rules, and tests around them, so a swap doesn't change what it's allowed to say.

What if a source turns out to be wrong?

Retire it and every answer that leaned on it changes. Sources are versioned, so an old guideline stops being cited the day the new one is approved.
